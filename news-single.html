  1. The Journey of a Kidney Cancer Patient

    My name is Herry Suryadi Sirath, and I am from Jakarta, Indonesia. On June 12, 2016, while I was shopping, I suddenly experienced chest tightness and difficulty breathing. After being examined by a local doctor, I was diagnosed with stage 4 kidney cancer, with a tumor measuring approximately 15 cm. Before this diagnosis, I was already familiar with cancer, as many of my family members had passed away from the disease. I also witnessed how surgery and chemoradiotherapy underwent by my father for nasopharyngeal cancer worsened his condition and eventually led to a recurrence. Therefore, when doctors in Jakarta advised me to undergo immediate surgery, I refused because I was afraid the outcome would be the same as what my family had experienced. I wanted to stay alive for my wife and children, so I was determined to look for a treatment option that did not involve surgery. During my search, a friend told me about **St. Stamford Modern Cancer Hospital Guangzhou** in China, which, according to him, uses minimally invasive treatments to manage cancer. This friend had previously recovered from breast cancer after receiving treatment at the hospital and is now in good health. This information gave me greater confidence and hope that I could receive more effective treatment with fewer side effects. On June 22, 2016, accompanied by my wife and child, I arrived at the hospital. Although I still felt a little anxious, the atmosphere there gave me renewed encouragement, as many other patients with even more severe conditions remained optimistic about their therapy. The doctors then developed a treatment plan tailored to my condition. I underwent a combination of cryosurgery and ozone therapy. The cryosurgery was performed under local anesthesia, so I remained conscious throughout the procedure and did not feel pain. After a CT scan to determine the exact location of the tumor, the doctors inserted six cryosurgery needles into the tumor. These needles released Argon and Helium gases to create extremely low temperatures capable of accurately destroying cancer cells and tumor tissue. According to the doctors, this method is suitable for both early-stage patients and those whose cancer has already spread, like me, offering effective results with minimal side effects. After the first treatment, I experienced noticeable improvement. My shortness of breath decreased, and my overall physical condition improved. I then underwent a second treatment session. Follow-up examinations showed that the cancer cells in my lungs were no longer detectable, and the kidney tumor, which had initially measured 15 cm, had shrunk to about 8 cm. I plan to continue with several additional minimally invasive therapies, believing that this treatment will help me achieve full recovery. Beyond the medical treatment, my experience at the hospital did not make me feel alone. On the ground floor, there is a coffee shop where patients from various countries gather, talk, encourage one another, and share their experiences in fighting cancer. There, I made many new friends who were also battling the disease, which made me realize that cancer is not something to face alone. It is important to remember that treatment outcomes may vary for each individual depending on their condition. This information is provided for reference only and should not be considered definitive medical guidance for others. For further information or direct consultation, readers are advised to contact the hospital through its online consultation service or the contact details available on its website.

  2. Vidi Aldiano Opens Up About Kidney Cancer

    Singer Vidi Aldiano has opened up about his current health condition. Vidi, who was diagnosed with kidney cancer in 2019, revealed that the cancer has continued to spread. Through an Instagram post, the singer—whose real name is Oxavia Aldiano—shared a moment of himself undergoing a chemotherapy session accompanied by his mother. Vidi referred to the therapy as a “spa day.” “Many people may not know that last year, this trust from God in the form of cancer had spread to several areas, which means I now have to schedule this ‘spa day’ appointment every three weeks,” Vidi wrote on Tuesday (September 19, 2023).

  3. The Story of a Man Diagnosed with Stage 4B Kidney Cancer Who Eventually Recovered

    A man from Bogor named Haidar Shofwan Ali Saepulloh shared his journey of battling stage 4B kidney cancer, specifically clear cell sarcoma, which was diagnosed during his childhood in 2011. At that time, he experienced symptoms such as swelling on the left side of his abdomen and pain in his back, which led him to undergo medical examinations and eventually receive a diagnosis of advanced-stage kidney cancer. After the diagnosis, Haidar had to go through a long and challenging treatment process, including 24 sessions of chemotherapy. He admitted that the biggest challenge during treatment was enduring the physical and mental side effects caused by chemotherapy. Throughout his journey, he emphasized that recovery depends not only on the patient but also on support from family and the surrounding environment. According to him, moral and emotional support from loved ones played a crucial role in helping cancer patients stay motivated and persist through lengthy treatments. Haidar also shared a message for children who are still fighting cancer, encouraging them not to give up and to continue their treatment consistently. He reminded the public that cancer is not a contagious disease, so patients should not be avoided or stigmatized. After a long struggle, consistent treatment, and strong support from those around him, Haidar was finally declared cancer-free in 2015 and now shares his story to motivate other cancer patients to remain optimistic during their recovery journey.
