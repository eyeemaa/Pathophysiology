What is this Kidney Cancer?

Kidney cancer is a condition in which cells in the kidneys grow abnormally and uncontrollably. This disease usually affects people over the age of 60, although in children it can appear in the form of Wilms' tumor. The kidneys are organs that function to filter metabolic waste from the blood and excrete it in the form of urine. In addition, the kidneys also produce the enzyme renin, which regulates blood pressure, and the hormone erythropoietin, which is involved in the formation of red blood cells.

About Kidney Cancer

Types of Kidney Cancer

Based on its characteristics, kidney cancer can be divided into several types, namely: Renal cell carcinoma
Urothelial carcinoma
Sarcoma
Wilm’s tumor.

Kidney Cancer Symptoms

If it has reached an advanced stage, the symptoms of kidney cancer that may appear are: A lump around the waist or abdomen Pain around the lower back and waist Persistent fever Excessive sweating, especially at night Unexplained weight loss

Kidney Cancer Diagnosis

These additional tests include: Blood tests, to measure the number of red blood cells, check kidney function, and tumor markers Urine tests, to detect infection or blood in the urine Imaging with ultrasound, CT scan, or MRI, to see the condition of the kidneys in more detail Kidney biopsy, to detect cancer cells by taking and examining a sample of kidney tissue

Kidney Cancer Prevention

Some efforts that can be made include: Quitting smoking Keeping blood pressure normal Maintaining an ideal weight Increasing the consumption of fruits and vegetables
